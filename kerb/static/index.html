Every verdict carries its evidence.

Kerb takes a list of local businesses — from a spreadsheet you already have, from Google Maps, or from OpenStreetMap — and tells you which ones are worth contacting, and why. Every score shows the measurement behind it. When Kerb cannot measure something, it says so rather than guessing.

Free and open source. It runs on your own machine, and your briefs and results never leave it.

01How it works Five steps, every business, every run

Kerb measures cheaply first and stops as soon as a business fails one of your conditions — so most are decided before anything slow or paid ever runs.

02What disqualifies —

Generated from the signal registry, so the builder can express everything a campaign file can. A disabled free signal is still measured — it just cannot reject anything. Paid signals stay off until you turn them on, because each one spends a request per business.

03How to rank what survives set

Normalised to 100 · drag to change · re-ranking after a survey is instant, because the measurements already exist.

04How much to collect no cap

A collector with no ceiling is how you wake up to a banned IP and an 80 GB temp folder. Every cap here is optional and every one stops the run cleanly — whatever was measured is kept, and the run is reported as partial rather than done.

Stop after this many results

Counts qualified businesses, not rows read. Blank means no cap.

Stop after this long

Minutes of wall clock. Useful when a source is slow rather than broken.

Stop after this many requests

Every network request, across sources and paid signals. The hard budget.

Workers

How many requests run at once. Lower it when a source starts throttling you.

OpenStreetMap tags advanced

Overrides the tags Kerb guesses from the trade. One per line, e.g. amenity=cafe.

05What to skip nothing

Prospecting is weekly. Without this, every run hands back the same people you called last time. A business whose verdict has changed since a previous run is always shown again — that is news, not a duplicate.

Already contacted / not a fit

A CSV, JSON or plain list of business ids. Applied before anything is measured, so it saves the requests too.

Seen in a previous run

Hides anything whose verdict is unchanged since that run. Changed verdicts still surface.

Forget after

A dated entry expires after this long, so someone you passed on last year can resurface.
